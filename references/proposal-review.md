# Proposal Review by the Technical-Architecture Role (when established)

For new product requirements, Communication first confirms its understanding of the intent with the customer (gate 0) and only then starts the Planning context. Planning produces the product proposal, architecture design, shared business contracts and acceptance criteria; Communication obtains the customer's proposal confirmation (gate 1). Only after that does the scheduler judge the team (keep, adjust boundaries, split, add or merge) by the [five-dimension standard and decision table](scheduler.md#五维判断与决策表) and complete the team plan; Communication obtains the customer's role-assignment confirmation (gate 2) before People Operations writes any role cards and before development is dispatched. Role-assignment and boundary changes are confirmed at gate 2. The technical review described here is performed by the project-management (technical-architecture) worker role only when the customer-confirmed team plan establishes it. When no such role exists, the scheduler checks the proposal against the confirmed planning proposal and architecture and the affected owners' findings before dispatch; no role invents a reviewer. Reuse valid existing proposals and same-object, same-version, same-scope confirmations; one gate's confirmation never substitutes for another. Do not rewrite historical technical proposals as planner work. Use this guide before presenting an application-code or test proposal for customer implementation approval. The reviewer is responsible for judging the proposal's quality and system impact, not merely forwarding it; the review is not an automatic permission to implement and does not replace any customer gate. Runtime contexts follow the fixed architecture: the customer window loads only Communication; Communication starts Planning, People Operations, Scheduler and Independent Acceptance as separate subagents; worker roles are dispatched only through the Scheduler.

## Submit and Review

For proposals that touch project engineering constraints, compare them with the current `docs/project-rules.md` maintained by the established project-management role when one exists, and identify any conflict or missing verification basis. Do not treat an absent or draft rule as an accepted constraint; record the gap for that owner (or Planning for product/architecture constraints) to resolve.

1. **Owner prepares the proposal.** The owning role investigates the actual entry, behavior and dependencies, then writes the relevant feature draft or dated design/change/fix proposal in its authorized scope. Identify the intended result, affected files and public contracts, dependencies, necessary versus optional changes, alternatives worth considering and validation. Send the document link and version to the established technical-review owner — the project-management role when the team plan created one, otherwise the scheduler for the pre-dispatch check — before requesting implementation approval from the customer. Read-only investigation, review and proposal refinement can proceed without code implementation approval, within existing assignment authorization.
2. **The reviewer evaluates the evidence.** Read the actual proposal and relevant code, contracts, architecture and feature documents. Check requirement fit against the confirmed planning proposal and architecture, five-layer rules, role ownership, reusable capabilities and unnecessary duplication. Trace relevant callers and consumers to assess changes to interfaces, shared data, errors, lifecycle and dependent tests. Compare realistic alternatives when they would improve simplicity, maintenance, cost or behavior; explain the tradeoff rather than inventing speculative options. A short contained change needs a proportionate review, not a mandatory meeting or new design document.
3. **Affected owners assess impact.** When another role's interface, data, behavior, implementation or verification is affected, obtain its scoped read-only assessment in an independent context bound to that role. Request concrete compatibility concerns, required caller/consumer changes, prerequisites and verification. Follow existing assignment authorization; reading a role card does not authorize dispatch. Use a requested or otherwise authorized [meeting](meeting.md) for unresolved disagreements, not for every review. An unavailable required assessment remains an explicit gap; do not claim no impact or invent that role's agreement.
4. **Conclude and return revisions.** Record one recommendation: `recommend approval`, `needs revision`, or `not ready`. Support it with findings, affected roles and their responses, material alternatives and unresolved questions. The original owner revises its proposal; the reviewer does not edit that owner's files or switch roles. The project-management role, when it is the reviewer, may revise its own implementation design within its scope. Recheck material revisions against the affected evidence and update the conclusion for the actual version.
5. **Present the approval brief.** Once the proposal is ready to recommend, give the customer the short decision brief below with a link to the full proposal and review. If not ready, explain the gap or decision needed; do not present it as a routine ready-to-implement approval. The customer may explicitly decide a documented tradeoff or unresolved risk; retain that decision and its conditions rather than silently relabeling missing evidence as verified.

When the reviewer's own shared-code or implementation proposal is under review, it receives the same substantive assessment. Obtain affected owners' feedback where needed and disclose the reviewer's authorship; do not invent an independent reviewer or a second project-management role. Scope/ownership changes still go to People Operations in its own context on the scheduler's team plan.

## Record and Brief

Write review findings in the reviewer role's existing authorized design/change/fix scope, linking the owner's canonical proposal/version rather than copying it. A bounded new review directory is not required. A proposal-level finding about the product plan or architecture belongs in that document's owner's scope; the reviewer reports it instead of editing it. If no review record path is authorized, provide a chat review and route any necessary scope update through the scheduler's team plan to People Operations; do not expand permission yourself. Keep customer authorization, the review recommendation, actual test results and independent acceptance conclusions as separate facts. A meeting's minutes may supply review evidence but consensus alone is not a completed review.

The customer-facing brief should be understandable without opening all technical records:

- **Problem and outcome:** What the user needs and what changes on success.
- **Recommendation and reason:** The reviewed approach and why it fits better than any material alternative.
- **Impact and cost:** Affected roles, required coordination, meaningful risks and verification; evidence supporting a contained change if no other role is affected.
- **Open decisions and options:** Unresolved disagreement, missing assessments and optional item IDs with benefits, costs and pending selection. Do not hide these to shorten the summary.
- **Approval requested:** Exact proposal link/version, implementation scope and option selection; distinguish approval to implement from a request to choose an alternative or resolve uncertainty.

Use a short paragraph or compact bullets, scaling detail to the decision. Summarize the reasoning, not just role names and technical headings. The brief points to the full proposal and review; it is not a second independently maintained specification.

## Implementation Gate and Changes

Before implementation dispatch, the scheduler checks that the reviewed proposal version matches the customer's confirmation and instruction to implement, the customer's role-assignment confirmation (gate 2), accepted role boundaries and actual task scope. The review recommendation does not grant authority or prove working behavior. Independent acceptance separately writes completion criteria from the confirmed planning proposal version and judges delivery; the scheduler cannot convert review approval into final acceptance. Reuse valid same-version, same-scope customer authorization; do not request duplicate approval because review or a later turn occurs.

Material changes to behavior, contracts, role impact, implementation scope or optional selection require a revised review and presentation of the affected decision before implementing it. An unchanged proposal does not require repeated review for every stage; recheck only when new evidence undermines the conclusion. Role isolation applies throughout: standalone owners hand off to a separate authorized review context, then receive the result in their original context. If no review context can be started, preserve the draft, report the missing review and continue authorized investigation/document preparation instead of self-reviewing as another role.

## Distinction from Planning Proposal Review

The review in this guide is the **implementation proposal review**. It is a different process from the pre-gate-1 [planning proposal review](planning-proposal-review.md), and neither substitutes for the other:

| Dimension | Implementation proposal review (this guide) | Planning proposal review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Object | An application-code or test implementation proposal, its implementation design and technical contracts; a feature draft or dated design/change/fix proposal | The planning proposal: product proposal, architecture design, shared business contracts and checkable acceptance criteria |
| Timing | After the team is established by the customer-confirmed team plan and before requesting the customer's implementation approval; after gate 1 | After Planning hands the proposal to Communication and before gate 1 |
| Reviewer | The project-management (technical-architecture) role when the team plan establishes it; otherwise the scheduler performs the pre-dispatch check against the confirmed planning proposal and architecture | The same Planning role in a separate read-only context, started by Communication |
| Record | A review record in the reviewer's existing authorized scope, linking the owner's proposal and version rather than copying it | A review record in Planning's document scope (`docs/planning/`, or the project-local override such as `角色卡/策划/docs/design/`), linking the reviewed proposal and version rather than copying it |
| Conclusion | `recommend approval`, `needs revision`, or `not ready` — a recommendation, not implementation authority | Three advisory recommendations defined solely in the planning proposal review guide; advice only, not a customer gate or implementation authority |

A planning proposal review never replaces the customer's gate 1 confirmation, and the customer's gate 1 confirmation of the planning proposal never replaces this implementation review. Neither review grants implementation authority or substitutes for independent acceptance. The object, timing, reviewer and conclusion types of this implementation proposal review are unchanged.
