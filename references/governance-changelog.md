## 2026-10-22 — Three customer gates for intent, proposal and role assignment

The customer added a process requirement: three independent customer gates, each requiring explicit customer consent before the workflow continues. **Gate 0 (intent confirmation)**: after intake, Communication writes its understanding of the goal, scope, priority, non-goals, authorized boundaries and known constraints and asks the customer to confirm it; Planning starts only after the customer confirms that understanding. **Gate 1 (proposal confirmation)**: after Planning completes the product proposal, architecture design and verifiable completion criteria, Communication presents them to the customer; no team planning or recruitment may start before the customer confirms the proposal. **Gate 2 (role-assignment confirmation)**: after the scheduler writes the team plan (which roles, who does what, their own scopes and write sets), Communication presents the role assignment to the customer; no role cards may be written and no development may be dispatched before the customer agrees. Communication is the only customer channel; customer agreement, refusal and change requests are recorded in `docs/communication/`. Default checked options, team consensus, a finished proposal or silence are never customer consent. A substantive intent or scope change reopens Gate 0, a substantive proposal change reopens Gate 1, and a role-assignment change reopens Gate 2. The rules were written into the role-definition references `communication-role.md`, `planning-role.md`, `scheduler.md`, `team-formation.md` and `acceptance-role.md`, and synchronized into this repository's Planning, Scheduler and Acceptance role cards and changelogs. Acceptance now judges against the customer-confirmed intent and proposal version, not against drafts or the unconfirmed team plan. The scheduler's team decision now follows a five-dimension check (responsibility independence, scope ownership, contract impact, evidence independence, coordination cost) and a decision table: reuse an existing role when it can carry the work and the scope already has an owner; change the boundary for the same responsibility with a new precise path; add a role only for a new, persistent, independent responsibility no existing role can carry; split only when an existing role is overloaded or mixed and the work is separable with disjoint write sets; merge or redraw overlapping roles so there is a single owner; assign or sequence one-off work without creating a role; name a single primary owner for a new shared abstraction; and prefer sequencing when a new role's coordination cost exceeds its benefit. The team plan must record the Section 1 coverage map, workload and scope reservation, contract and evidence impact, coordination cost, options considered, rationale, precise boundary-change list and the Gate 2 confirmation state. The scheduler is the requester of the capability gap and boundaries, not the role-card author: the team plan states the capability gap, responsibility goal, exact write boundary and write set, dependencies and staffing/task assignment, and does not write role-card capability descriptions. People Operations is the capability-description completer and card writer, not the recruitment decision-maker: it must add what the role can do, the knowledge and skills required, its distinction from existing roles, plus responsibilities, required reading, handoff inputs/outputs and execution principles, then apply the exact boundary to `boundary.md` / `boundary.yaml`, check non-overlapping permissions and update the changelog. It cannot merely copy boundaries into Section 1. If the plan lacks capability detail, People Operations reports the gap to the scheduler and does not fill the plan itself or pre-build cards. No roles, role IDs, groups, boundaries, default write scopes or program permissions changed; shared entry, format and promotion documents remain untouched.

## 2026-10-22 — Five built-in management roles

The customer approved five built-in management roles: Communication, Planning, Team Formation (recruitment; now People Operations), Scheduler and Independent Acceptance. They are available immediately in any project, need no project role card or prior recruitment, and hold narrow default write scopes: `docs/communication/`, `docs/planning/`, `角色卡/` role governance artifacts, `docs/scheduling/` and `docs/acceptance/`. The corrected workflow runs Communication intake (requirements written to `docs/communication/`) → Planning (product proposal, architecture design and shared business contracts written to `docs/planning/`) → Scheduler judges the team (unchanged, new hires, or role split) → customer confirmation/implementation authorization through Communication. When unchanged, the scheduler dispatches development after that authorization; when changed, the scheduler writes the team plan and role-adjustment proposal to `docs/scheduling/` and hands it to Team Formation, which writes or changes only the role cards and boundaries listed in that plan. The scheduler then dispatches development after the authorization; workers report to the scheduler; Independent Acceptance judges against the planning proposal. Architecture design belongs to Planning; project management is not a default role and is created only when the scheduler's plan says implementation truly needs a technical-architecture work role. Worker roles still require accepted cards and Section 1 boundaries.

Two hard constraints were confirmed. First, Team Formation must not recruit on its own: the scheduler's written plan in `docs/scheduling/` is the only basis for creating or changing role cards; without it, Team Formation stops and reports the gap, adds no unlisted roles, expands no permissions, and returns infeasible or incomplete plans to the scheduler rather than editing them or pre-building cards. Second, Independent Acceptance must judge against the confirmed planning proposal (product proposal plus architecture design), which must contain checkable completion criteria — scope, success/failure behavior, important boundary cases, architecture constraints and required evidence. Acceptance writes criteria before implementation from the confirmed plan version, judges the current candidate against the same version, never invents standards or adds requirements, and returns plans lacking verifiable criteria to Planning. The scheduler hands the current candidate, plan version and raw evidence to acceptance and releases only a pass that matches the current candidate.

The customer also corrected the ownership principle: role definitions are governance materials, not role-owned documents. Role-definition references — the five management role guides, the worker role guides (`project-role`, `test-role`, `built-in-assembly`), the built-in boundary pair and this changelog — are maintained by Team Formation alone.
